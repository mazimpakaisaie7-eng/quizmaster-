General Knowledge • Lesson 1

Introduction to General Knowledge

General knowledge includes facts and information from many areas of life. It can include the names of countries, scientific discoveries, historical events, sports, technology, and culture.

Why is general knowledge important?

  • It helps us understand our surroundings.
  • It improves communication and learning.
  • It encourages curiosity and asking questions.
  • It helps us connect ideas from different subjects.
  • It supports informed decision-making.

How to improve general knowledge

  1. Read reliable educational books and articles.
  2. Study different subjects regularly.
  3. Ask questions when information is unclear.
  4. Discuss what you learn with others.
  5. Practise with educational questions and explanations.
General Knowledge • Lesson 2

Geography: Understanding the Earth

Geography is the study of the Earth, its physical features, people, places, climate, and environments. It helps us understand where places are located and how people interact with their surroundings.

The seven continents

In the commonly used seven-continent model, the continents are:

  1. Africa
  2. Asia
  3. Europe
  4. North America
  5. South America
  6. Antarctica
  7. Australia

The five oceans

The five commonly recognised oceans are: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, and Arctic. Oceans support marine life, influence climate, and provide routes for transport and trade.

Countries and capital cities

A country is a territory with its own government. A capital city is generally the main location of a country's national government.

Examples include Kigali in Rwanda, Nairobi in Kenya, Kampala in Uganda, Dodoma in Tanzania, and Bujumbura in Burundi.

Compass directions

The four main compass directions are North, South, East, and West. They help people describe locations and read maps.

Remember: Geography helps us understand the relationship between people, places, and the natural environment.

General Knowledge • Lesson 3

Science: Understanding Nature

Science is the systematic study of the natural world through observation, investigation, questions, and evidence.

Biology

Biology is the study of living organisms, including humans, animals, plants, and microorganisms.

Physics

Physics studies matter, energy, motion, forces, light, and sound.

Gravity is the attraction between objects that have mass. Earth's gravity helps keep people and objects on its surface.

Chemistry

Chemistry studies substances, their properties, their structures, and how they change.

Water is a chemical compound made from hydrogen and oxygen. Its chemical formula is H₂O.

States of matter

  • Solid: Has a definite shape and volume.
  • Liquid: Has a definite volume but takes the shape of its container.
  • Gas: Spreads to fill the available space.
General Knowledge • Lesson 4

The Human Body

The human body contains organs and systems that work together to support life.

  • Brain: Helps control body activities, thinking, and memory.
  • Heart: Pumps blood around the body.
  • Lungs: Help exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide.
  • Stomach: Helps break down food during digestion.
  • Kidneys: Filter waste from the blood and help regulate body fluids.
  • Skeleton: Supports the body and protects important organs.
  • Skin: Forms a protective covering around the body.

Body systems

The circulatory system transports blood. The respiratory system supports breathing. The digestive system processes food. The nervous system carries messages throughout the body.

General Knowledge • Lesson 5

Technology and Computers

Technology includes tools, systems, and methods that people develop to solve problems and perform tasks.

Computer basics

  • Hardware: Physical parts of a computer.
  • Software: Programs and instructions used by a device.
  • Input: Data entered into a computer.
  • Output: Information produced by a computer.
  • Storage: Keeping information for later use.

The Internet

The Internet is a worldwide network of connected computer networks. It allows people to communicate, study, exchange information, and use online services.

Digital safety

  • Use strong and unique passwords.
  • Do not share private account details.
  • Be careful with unexpected links and attachments.
  • Check information before sharing it.
  • Keep devices and applications updated.
General Knowledge • Lesson 6

History: Learning from the Past

History is the study of past events and human societies. It helps us understand how communities and countries have changed over time.

Historical sources

  • Written sources: Books, letters, and official documents.
  • Oral sources: Stories and accounts passed between people.
  • Archaeological sources: Objects, buildings, and remains from the past.
  • Visual sources: Photographs, paintings, and maps.

Why study history?

  • To understand how societies developed.
  • To preserve cultural heritage.
  • To learn how past events influenced the present.
  • To evaluate evidence and different historical accounts.
General Knowledge • Lesson 7

Sports and Physical Education

Sports are organised activities that involve physical skills, practice, rules, and sometimes competition.

Examples of sports

  • Football
  • Basketball
  • Volleyball
  • Athletics
  • Tennis
  • Swimming

Why are sports important?

  • They encourage teamwork and cooperation.
  • They develop coordination and physical skills.
  • They teach respect for rules and other people.
  • They create opportunities for social interaction.

Learning about sports also includes understanding their rules, equipment, history, and terminology.

General Knowledge • Lesson 8

Learning About Rwanda

Rwanda is a landlocked country in East Africa. Its capital city is Kigali.

Geography

Rwanda is known for its hills, lakes, green landscapes, and diverse natural environments. It shares borders with Uganda, Tanzania, Burundi, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Official languages

Rwanda's official languages are Kinyarwanda, English, French, and Kiswahili.

National learning topics

Learning about Rwanda can include its geography, history, national symbols, culture, public institutions, economy, and development.

Study tip: Use reliable educational books and official publications when learning about national institutions and information that may change.

General Knowledge • Lesson 9

Environment and Nature

The environment includes living organisms, land, water, air, and the conditions in which people and other organisms live.

Natural resources

Natural resources include water, soil, forests, sunlight, minerals, and wildlife. People depend on these resources for many needs.

Protecting the environment

  • Dispose of waste responsibly.
  • Protect trees and natural habitats.
  • Use water carefully.
  • Reduce unnecessary pollution.
  • Support responsible use of natural resources.
General Knowledge • Lesson 10

Basic Mathematics

Mathematics is the study of numbers, quantities, patterns, shapes, and relationships.

Four basic operations

  • Addition: Combining quantities. Example: 4 + 3 = 7.
  • Subtraction: Finding the difference. Example: 9 - 5 = 4.
  • Multiplication: Repeated addition. Example: 3 × 4 = 12.
  • Division: Sharing into equal groups. Example: 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

Mathematics is useful in shopping, measuring, planning, science, technology, and everyday life.

🚦 Road Safety Lessons

Road safety is the practice of using roads responsibly to reduce danger and protect drivers, passengers, cyclists, motorcyclists, and pedestrians.

Everyone using the road has a responsibility to pay attention, follow applicable traffic rules, and respect other road users.

Road Safety • Lesson 1

Introduction to Road Safety

Road safety means using roads in a careful, responsible, and lawful way. It involves understanding traffic signs, obeying road rules, and paying attention to other road users.

Who uses the road?

  • Drivers of cars, buses, and trucks.
  • Motorcyclists and passengers.
  • Cyclists.
  • Pedestrians.
  • People using other permitted forms of transport.

Why is road safety important?

  • It helps prevent road crashes.
  • It protects lives and reduces injuries.
  • It encourages respect between road users.
  • It supports orderly movement of traffic.

Remember: Road safety is everyone's responsibility, not only the responsibility of drivers.

Road Safety • Lesson 2

Understanding Traffic Signs

Traffic signs are symbols, shapes, colours, and words placed near roads to communicate information and instructions to road users.

Why are traffic signs important?

  • They warn people about possible dangers.
  • They communicate restrictions and instructions.
  • They provide directions and useful information.
  • They help organise road traffic.

Main groups of traffic signs

  • Warning signs: Alert road users to possible hazards.
  • Regulatory signs: Communicate rules, restrictions, or obligations.
  • Information signs: Provide directions and useful information.

The exact appearance and meaning of a sign should be learned from the official road signs used in the relevant country.

Road Safety • Lesson 3

Warning Traffic Signs

Warning signs alert road users to conditions that may require extra attention or caution. They help people prepare for possible hazards.

Examples of warning signs

  • Sharp bend: Warns that the road changes direction sharply.
  • Road narrows: Warns that the available road width reduces.
  • Pedestrian crossing: Alerts drivers to a place where pedestrians may cross.
  • Children crossing: Warns that children may be near or crossing the road.
  • Slippery road: Warns about a surface that may reduce tyre grip.
  • Road works: Warns that construction or maintenance may affect the road.
  • Steep hill: Warns about a significant uphill or downhill section.

How should road users respond?

Pay attention to the warning, observe the road, and follow the applicable rules. Drivers should adjust their driving appropriately to the conditions and avoid sudden or unsafe actions.

Road Safety • Lesson 4

Regulatory Traffic Signs

Regulatory signs communicate traffic rules, restrictions, and obligations. Road users must understand and obey the applicable instructions.

Examples

  • STOP: Requires a driver to stop as instructed before proceeding safely.
  • Give Way: Requires a driver to yield priority as indicated.
  • No Entry: Indicates that vehicles must not enter that road or area.
  • Speed limit: Shows the maximum permitted speed for that section.
  • No Parking: Indicates that parking is prohibited in the marked area.
  • No Overtaking: Restricts overtaking where the sign applies.

Important: Do not rely on memory alone when interpreting an unfamiliar sign. Learn the official sign meanings and follow instructions displayed on the actual road.

Road Safety • Lesson 5

Information and Direction Signs

Information signs help road users find places, understand road facilities, and follow directions.

Examples

  • Direction signs showing routes to destinations.
  • Signs identifying towns and localities.
  • Signs indicating hospitals or health facilities.
  • Signs showing parking facilities.
  • Signs identifying public services.
  • Signs providing route and distance information.

Drivers should read signs carefully while maintaining attention to the road. Passengers can also help with navigation when appropriate.

Road Safety • Lesson 6

Traffic Lights and Their Meanings

Traffic lights control the movement of road users at intersections and other locations.

Common traffic light colours

  • Red: Stop and wait.
  • Amber or yellow: Prepare to stop where it is safe and follow the applicable signal rules.
  • Green: Proceed only when the way is clear and it is safe and permitted to do so.

Important safety points

  • Do not enter an intersection when it is blocked.
  • Watch for pedestrians and other road users.
  • Do not assume other road users will always obey signals.
  • Follow traffic officers' lawful directions where applicable.

Remember: A green light does not mean you should proceed without checking that the way is safe.

Road Safety • Lesson 7

Road Markings and Their Meanings

Road markings are lines, symbols, and other marks placed on the road surface to guide and regulate traffic.

Examples of road markings

  • Centre line: Separates traffic moving in opposite directions on some roads.
  • Lane lines: Help separate lanes travelling in the same direction.
  • Stop line: Indicates where a vehicle should stop when required.
  • Pedestrian crossing: Marks an area intended for pedestrian crossing.
  • Directional arrows: Indicate permitted or required directions of travel.
  • Parking markings: Identify designated parking spaces or restrictions.

Drivers should observe road markings and follow the traffic rules that apply to them. Markings should never be ignored simply because there is no nearby sign.

Road Safety • Lesson 8

Right of Way and Giving Priority

Right of way refers to which road user has priority to proceed in a particular situation. Traffic signs, signals, road markings, and applicable traffic rules determine priority.

Important principles

  • Obey STOP and Give Way signs.
  • Follow traffic lights and lawful directions from traffic officers.
  • Pay attention to pedestrians at crossings.
  • Approach intersections carefully.
  • Do not force your way into traffic.
  • Never assume another road user has seen you.

Safety first: Priority rules can depend on the road layout and local law. Learn the rules applicable in Rwanda from official materials.

Road Safety • Lesson 9

Pedestrian Safety

Pedestrians are people travelling on foot. They are among the road users who need particular protection.

Safe pedestrian habits

  • Use pavements or designated walking areas where available.
  • Use designated pedestrian crossings where provided.
  • Look carefully for traffic before crossing.
  • Follow pedestrian signals where available.
  • Avoid distractions while crossing.
  • Be especially careful near schools and busy roads.
  • Help children and people who need assistance to cross safely.

Pedestrian crossings

A pedestrian crossing is a designated place where people can cross the road. Drivers should observe crossing rules and take care around people using the crossing.

Road Safety • Lesson 10

Safe Driving and Driver Responsibilities

Safe driving requires attention, patience, knowledge of traffic rules, and respect for all road users.

Responsible driving habits

  • Hold the appropriate valid driving licence.
  • Ensure the vehicle is roadworthy.
  • Wear a seat belt and ensure passengers use appropriate restraints.
  • Obey road signs, signals, and speed restrictions.
  • Keep a safe distance from other vehicles.
  • Use indicators when required and appropriate.
  • Check mirrors and surroundings before manoeuvres.
  • Never drive when impaired by alcohol, drugs, or fatigue.
  • Avoid using a handheld phone while driving.

Vehicle maintenance

Regular vehicle checks help identify problems that may affect safety. Important components include brakes, tyres, lights, mirrors, steering, and other safety equipment.

Road Safety • Lesson 11

Motorcycle Safety

Motorcycles require careful handling because riders are more exposed than people inside enclosed vehicles.

Safety principles

  • Wear an approved and properly fitted helmet.
  • Use appropriate protective clothing.
  • Ensure the motorcycle is in good condition.
  • Obey traffic signs and speed restrictions.
  • Make yourself visible to other road users.
  • Avoid carrying more passengers than legally permitted.
  • Never ride while impaired or distracted.

Passengers should also use appropriate protective equipment and follow safety instructions.

Road Safety • Lesson 12

Bicycle and Cyclist Safety

Cyclists are road users and should understand the rules that apply to bicycles on public roads.

Safe cycling habits

  • Keep the bicycle in safe working condition.
  • Use appropriate lights and reflectors where required.
  • Stay alert and watch for vehicles and pedestrians.
  • Use permitted cycling areas where available.
  • Signal intentions when required and safe.
  • Obey traffic signs and signals.
  • Avoid carrying loads that make the bicycle unstable.
Road Safety • Lesson 13

Weather, Visibility, and Road Conditions

Rain, fog, darkness, dust, and poor road conditions can make travelling more difficult. Road users should adapt their behaviour to the conditions.

When visibility is poor

  • Use appropriate vehicle lights.
  • Reduce speed when conditions require it.
  • Increase the distance from vehicles ahead.
  • Watch for pedestrians and cyclists.
  • Avoid sudden movements.

When roads are wet

Wet surfaces can reduce tyre grip and increase stopping distance. Drivers should be cautious, avoid abrupt braking where possible, and leave sufficient space.

Road Safety • Lesson 14

Road Emergencies and Crashes

An emergency on the road requires calm, responsible action. Protecting people and contacting appropriate emergency services are important priorities.

General safety principles

  • Keep yourself and others away from immediate danger where possible.
  • Contact the appropriate emergency services.
  • Give clear information about the location and situation.
  • Do not create additional danger around the scene.
  • Follow instructions from emergency responders.
  • Do not move an injured person unnecessarily unless there is immediate danger.

Important: In a real emergency, seek help from trained emergency responders. Do not attempt actions beyond your training.

Road Safety • Lesson 15

Respect, Responsibility, and Road Discipline

Good road behaviour includes patience, respect, attention, and following the law. Road users should consider the safety of everyone around them.

Examples of responsible behaviour

  • Respect other road users.
  • Do not drive aggressively.
  • Do not misuse the horn.
  • Follow traffic officers' lawful instructions.
  • Do not block pedestrian crossings.
  • Keep roads and public spaces free from litter.
  • Report dangerous road conditions through appropriate channels.

Remember: A responsible road user protects not only themselves but also other people.

Important Note About Road Rules in Rwanda

These lessons provide general road safety education. They are not a replacement for Rwanda's current traffic laws, official road regulations, driving instruction, or guidance from competent authorities.

Exact speed limits, penalties, licence requirements, vehicle requirements, and other legal details must be checked against current official Rwandan regulations.

Always follow the traffic signs, road markings, and lawful instructions that apply where you are travelling.

How to Study Effectively

Learning becomes more useful when you understand ideas instead of only memorising answers.

1. Choose one topic

Start with one lesson, such as traffic signs or geography. Avoid trying to study everything at once.

2. Read carefully

Read the explanation and identify the important ideas, definitions, and examples.

3. Explain what you learned

Try to explain the lesson using your own words. This helps you check your understanding.

4. Practise with questions

Use the Quiz Master quiz to test your knowledge. Read the explanations after answering.

5. Review difficult topics

If you find a question difficult, return to the related lesson and study it again.

Learning principle: Read, understand, practise, review, and keep improving. A good score is useful, but understanding is even more important.
